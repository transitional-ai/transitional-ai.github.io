# Research

## Research Themes

I lead a transdisciplinary research program at the intersection of **artificial intelligence, data science, and scientific and cultural applications**. Our research develops robust and uncertainty-aware AI methods and applies them to challenges in **Earth and space sciences, climate, mineral exploration, language, culture, and human-centred AI**.

A central focus is the development of **deep-learning and Bayesian frameworks for uncertainty-aware decision-making**, complemented by research on foundation models, multimodal AI, and responsible AI.

Our research is organised around three interconnected themes:

1. **[AI Methodologies and Uncertainty](#1-ai-methodologies-and-uncertainty)**
2. **[AI for Earth, Climate and Space](#2-ai-for-earth-climate-and-space)**
3. **[Digital Humanities and Human-Centred AI](#3-digital-humanities-and-human-centred-ai)**

---

## 1. AI Methodologies and Uncertainty

We develop machine-learning methods for robust prediction, representation learning, and decision-making, with particular emphasis on **Bayesian deep learning, neuroevolution, ensemble learning, data augmentation, and uncertainty quantification**.

Our early work developed neuroevolutionary methods for dynamic time-series forecasting and modular pattern recognition ([Chandra et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2017.02.065); [Chandra et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asoc.2018.05.041); [Chandra and Cripps, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2018.08.011)). We subsequently developed GAN-based data augmentation for limited and imbalanced datasets ([Sharma et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3158977)) and methods for extreme-event forecasting ([Hua et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.02407)).

A major research direction is **Bayesian deep learning**, including scalable Bayesian neural networks using parallel-tempering MCMC and parallel computing ([Chandra et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2019.05.082)), subsequently extended to Bayesian graph neural networks, autoencoders, and transfer learning ([Chandra et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3111898); [Chandra et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3163270); [Chandra and Kapoor, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2019.10.042)).

Current work includes **uncertainty-aware data imputation**, spatiotemporal modelling, and the development of **BayesClustering** for uncertainty-aware clustering and representation learning. We are also exploring the integration of Bayesian inference with **multimodal and foundation models**.

## 2. AI for Earth, Climate and Space

We apply machine learning, Bayesian inference, and remote sensing to **environmental modelling, mineral exploration, climate extremes, and planetary science**.

Our work has developed machine-learning approaches for geological lineament detection and mineral exploration from satellite imagery ([Farahbakhsh et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2019.1674462)) and deep-learning approaches for lithological mapping ([Shirmard et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14040819)). Current research combines satellite imagery, geochemical observations, and spatial machine learning for **critical-mineral exploration**, together with open-source land-cover mapping tools for Fiji ([Gurjar et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asr.2026.03.024)).

We have also developed machine-learning methods for **climate extremes, tropical cyclone forecasting, floods, and streamflow modelling** ([Chandra and Dayal, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2015.7257103); [Deo and Chandra, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-29911-8_22); [Sutar and Chandra, 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.08397); [Kapoor and Chandra, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2025.134434)).

We also developed the **Bayeslands** and **Bayesreef** frameworks for Bayesian inference and uncertainty quantification in geoscientific models of landscape and reef evolution ([Chandra et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2019.06.012); [Chandra et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-2959-2020)).

More recently, we have extended this research to **planetary exploration**, developing deep-learning methods for crater detection on the Moon and Mars ([Ma et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44453-026-00036-x)) and unsupervised learning for mineral mapping from Martian imagery ([Lovelock and Chandra, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17213578)).

Current research investigates **multimodal AI for planetary science**, combining orbital imagery, terrain models, geological information, and scientific literature to analyse Martian environments, including dust storms, geology, mineral resources, and candidate landing sites.

Our long-term goal is to develop **trustworthy AI systems that support autonomous scientific discovery and future robotic and human exploration of the Moon and Mars**.

## 3. Digital Humanities and Human-Centred AI

We explore how **large language models and foundation models can support the study of language, culture, philosophy, religion, cinema, and music**, while developing human-centred approaches to the responsible use of AI.

Our research has developed frameworks for evaluating machine translation across **Sanskrit, Mandarin, and Indian languages**, comparing human translation with Google Translate and large language models ([Shukla et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlp.2023.100025); [Wang et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlp.2025.100188); [Chandra et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3585629)).

We pioneered computational approaches to **ancient religious and philosophical texts**, including the Bhagavad Gita and Upanishads ([Chandra and Kulkarni, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3152266); [Chandra and Ranjan, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0273476)), and developed methods for metaphor detection across religious texts ([Chandra et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3411060)).

Current work includes **Vedanta-RAG**, retrieval-augmented generation for Hindu philosophical knowledge, together with research on hallucination, retrieval, and factual grounding in language models.

We also apply language models to **cinema and music**, including longitudinal analysis of violence and narrative patterns in Hollywood movies ([Chandra and Ren, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mlwa.2025.100749)).

A related focus is **human-centred and responsible AI**, examining how language models represent and reason about concepts such as fairness, personality, consciousness, values, and ethical decision-making. Current research explores philosophical concepts including **Ahimsa and Nyaya** as potential foundations for ethical decision-making in agentic AI.

Together, this research develops computational approaches to human culture while investigating how AI systems can remain **grounded in human values, cultural knowledge, and responsible decision-making**.

---

## Research Vision

Our goal is to develop **trustworthy, uncertainty-aware, and human-centred AI systems** that combine data, scientific knowledge, and human perspectives.

By bringing together advances in **Bayesian AI, deep learning, foundation models, remote sensing, and computational humanities**, we aim to develop AI that can learn from diverse sources, quantify uncertainty, and support reliable human decision-making across **scientific, environmental, cultural, and societal domains**.
